Helambu & the Valley

An 11-day plan for you, your 7-year-old daughter and your brother: four days in the Kathmandu Valley, a five-day jeep-and-walk loop through the Hyolmo villages of Tarkeghyang and Melamchi Ghyang, and a spare day before the flight home.

Overview

The shape of the trip

Helambu sits about 35 km north-east of Boudha in a straight line, but the road climbs and winds, so the drive takes most of a day. The plan does the valley first. That gives jet lag a few days to clear and lets everyone spend a few nights at 1,400 m before going higher. The last valley day is a buffer in case the mountain road is slow.

  1. DAY1

    Arrive, get set up, first walk around Boudha

    Sleep: Boudha, ~1,400 mAirport → Boudha ≈ 20–30 min
    • At the airport, in this order: visa on arrival (form, then pay at the bank counter, then the immigration desk), then the SIM counters in arrivals, then cash. Details in Logistics and Money & SIM.
    • Boudhanath is only 5–7 km from the airport, which is one reason it makes a good base. It also sits on the road out toward Helambu.

    For herLong flights plus a 12¾-hour time jump from California (13¾ in winter; Nepal runs on UTC+5:45) make Day 1 a write-off. Keep it to food, a short walk and bed.

  2. DAY2

    A full day in Boudha

    Entry NPR 400 per adult · under-10s free
    • Morning kora around the stupa, when monks, grandmothers and pigeons are all out. Have breakfast on a rooftop facing the dome.
    • Shechen Monastery is a few minutes' walk away. Take shoes off at the door and walk clockwise inside.
    • Optional: a 15-minute taxi to Kopan Monastery on the hill to the north, for valley views and a quiet garden.
    • Helambu errands: confirm the jeep and guide, buy the Langtang National Park permits (or let the agency do it), pull cash for the hills, buy snacks your daughter will actually eat.
    For herLet her buy a small string of prayer flags here. She can tie it at the top of Ama Yangri on Day 7, where pilgrims leave flags and stones.

  3. DAY3

    Bhaktapur, then Kathmandu University

    Bhaktapur entry NPR 1,800 · under-10s freeCarry passports

    Bhaktapur and Kathmandu University are on the same road east (the Araniko Highway). KU's campus is in Dhulikhel, about 28 km and 40 minutes from the airport, so pairing it with Bhaktapur saves a separate trip. If your brother has a reason to see KU on the Boudha day instead, swap it.

    • Bhaktapur, morning: Durbar Square, then Taumadhi Square with Nyatapola, the five-tiered temple. Walk on to Pottery Square to watch the wheels and the drying pots.
    • Eat juju dhau ("king curd"), the thick sweet yogurt sold in clay pots. It's only made here.
    • Afternoon: drive on to Dhulikhel and walk the KU campus. Ask your brother whether visitors need to sign in at the gate.
    • Stay for late-afternoon light in Dhulikhel if the sky is clear; the ridge looks north toward the Himalaya. Expect slow traffic back.
    For herNyatapola's staircase has pairs of guardian statues on each level, from wrestlers up to lions, elephants and goddesses. Let her count them on the way up.

  4. DAY4

    Patan, the old heart of Lalitpur

    Durbar Square + Patan Museum NPR 1,000 · under-10s free

    Patan and Lalitpur are the same city. Patan is the historic core of Lalitpur Metropolitan City, so one day covers both.

    • Patan Durbar Square and the Patan Museum inside the old palace; one ticket covers both. The museum's courtyard café is a good mid-morning break.
    • Golden Temple (Kwa Bahal), a short walk north, has its own small fee.
    • Afternoon: the Central Zoo at Jawalakhel, about 1.5 km west. Dinner in Jhamsikhel.
    • Tonight: pack small bags for Helambu and leave the big luggage at the hotel.

  1. DAY5

    Jeep to Tarkeghyang

    Sleep: Tarkeghyang, 2,600 mDrive 5–6 h+1,200 m in one day
    • Leave Boudha by 7 am. Most drivers stop for lunch in Melamchi Bazaar, the last real town, and arrive early afternoon. The upper road is dirt and needs 4WD.
    • Have permits and passports ready for park checks.
    • Afternoon: settle in, then walk slowly to Gekye Gompa, the old village monastery. Nothing strenuous on arrival day.
    • Tarkeghyang is a stone-built Hyolmo village. Hyolmo is the local Tibetan Buddhist community; you'll hear Yolmo and Nepali spoken.
    For herThis is the big altitude jump. At dinner, watch her appetite, mood and energy compared with the valley days. A headache plus poor appetite or nausea tonight means tomorrow stays easy. See Altitude.
    Tarkeghyang on mapPhotos ↗
  2. DAY6

    Cave day around Tarkeghyang

    Sleep: TarkeghyangShort or half day

    This is your "walk to a cave" day, placed before Ama Yangri on purpose: a second night at 2,600 m before the summit is the best altitude medicine there is.

    • Guru Rinpoche & Yeshe Tsogyal caves (short, ideal with a 7-year-old). From the gompa, contour along the south-west side of the village, cross a gully, pass through a road cutting to a stream turning water-powered prayer wheels, then climb about 30 m through forest. The first cave is a dark opening under a fallen boulder, with a short passage, a one-metre step down and a low crawl to a second entrance. Small clay offerings (tsa-tsa) fill the cave. Bring headlamps.
    • Milarepa's Cave (optional, longer). Tak Phuk Senge Dzong, the "Tiger Cave of the Lion Fortress", sits roughly 1.2 km south-west and some 600 m below the village, at about 2,000 m. The path winds down through terraced fields and steep forest to a small gompa built in 1991. The cave behind it is enclosed and kept locked, so ask your lodge or guide to arrange the caretaker before you go. Remember that every metre down is a metre back up.
    • Early dinner and early bed for summit day.
    For herThe crawl-through cave will likely be her favorite moment of the trip. Let her lead with the headlamp. Remind her that caves and offerings are sacred: look, don't touch.
    Guru Rinpoche cavesMilarepa's Cave
  3. DAY7

    Ama Yangri, 3,771 m

    Sleep: back in TarkeghyangSummit 12,372 ftGo on a weekday

    Ama Yangri is the guardian goddess of the Hyolmo valley and the highest point around. The top has a small gompa, Ama Yangri Zangdo Palri, prayer flags and a wide Himalayan view: Langtang, Ganesh Himal, Dorje Lakpa, Jugal.

    • Plan A, best with a 7-year-old: jeep at first light from Tarkeghyang up the dirt road to Chomothang base camp (about 3,200 m). From there it's about 2.5 km and roughly 570 m of steady climbing, which takes adults about 2.5–3 hours. Budget 3–4 hours up for her, then descend and ride back down.
    • Plan B: walk the whole way from the village. That's 6–8 hours round trip with no tea shops on the trail, too long for most 7-year-olds.
    • Mornings are clearest. Aim to reach the top by mid-morning and set a firm turnaround time (11:00 is sensible) whatever happens.
    • The hike has become a popular weekend trip from Kathmandu, so pick a weekday if you can.
    • Carry everything you'll need: water, lunch, warm layers, sun protection. Base camp has only a few simple lodges.

    Turn around if she has

    a headache that doesn't ease with rest, water and a snack; nausea or vomiting; unusual tiredness or crankiness; or any clumsiness, stumbling or confusion. Going down fixes almost all of it. You sleep 1,170 m lower tonight either way.

    For herChildren do summit this. The Himalayan Times reported a four-year-old and a 60-year-old grandmother making it to the top. Pace is everything: tiny steps, snack every 30 minutes, and let her set the speed.

  4. DAY8

    Walk to Melamchi Ghyang

    Sleep: Melamchi Ghyang, 2,530 m6.2 km · 4–5 h adult pace

    The two villages face each other across the Melamchi Khola. A family-trek operator lists the walk at 6.2 km and 4–5 hours. With a child, plan on 5–6 hours including lunch, and start by 8 am.

    • About 2 hours of steep descent through Hyolmo hamlets to the suspension bridge over the Melamchi Khola, with a large chorten nearby.
    • About 2 hours of steep climbing through rhododendron forest, past many chortens, up to Melamchi Ghyang.
    • Trekking poles save knees on the way down. Hold her hand on the bridge.
    • Afternoon: the Amitabha temple, which displays Guru Rinpoche's "jewel millstone".
    • If she's spent after Ama Yangri: take a rest day in Tarkeghyang instead and skip the overnight in Melamchi Ghyang, or have the jeep drive you round. A road reaches Melamchi Ghyang, but it's long and bumpy.
    Melamchi Ghyang on mapPhotos ↗
  5. DAY9

    The sun-and-moon cave, then down to Kathmandu

    Sleep: BoudhaDrive 5–6 h
    • Nyinda Rangjon Phuk, the "practice cave of the self-arisen sun and moon", is where tradition says Guru Rinpoche meditated. It's at the forest edge above the village gompa, along a walled path. It's a huge rock slab resting on boulders, inside a stone-walled compound with a locked gate. A villager nearby keeps the key, so ask at your lodge the evening before. There's also a holy pond to visit.
    • Jeep pickup late morning. You'll be back in Boudha by evening.

Where to stay

One valley base, simple lodges in the hills

Use one base in Kathmandu so you unpack once. Boudha is the practical choice: close to the airport, on the road toward Helambu, and the stupa is lovely to come home to. Logistics

Jeep, permits, guide, visa

The jeep

  • Best option: one 4WD jeep with driver for all five Helambu days. The driver stays in the villages and you cover his food and bed. That gives you the dawn ride to Ama Yangri base camp and a pickup in Melamchi Ghyang without extra phone calls.
  • Cheaper option: a one-way drop on Day 5 and a separate pickup on Day 9, plus a local jeep from Tarkeghyang to base camp on Day 7.
  • Price guide: in 2023 a private vehicle from Kathmandu to Melamchi Ghyang cost about NPR 16,000 one way. October–November is peak season and adds 10–20%. Let your brother get two or three quotes.
  • Route: drivers go either the long, mostly paved way through Dhulikhel and Melamchi Bazaar or the shorter, rougher way via Sankhu. Let the driver choose based on current road conditions.
  • Avoid the monsoon (June–September). Landslides close this road; the 2021 Melamchi flood badly damaged the valley.

Permits and a licensed guide

Langtang National Park entry, per foreign adultNPR 3,000 + 13% VAT = 3,390 Your daughter (under 10)Free · bring her passport TIMS trekker cardAgency will advise
  • Tarkeghyang, Melamchi Ghyang and Ama Yangri are all inside or at the edge of Langtang National Park. Buy permits at the Nepal Tourism Board office (Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu) or at the park entry point. Your brother needs one too unless he's a Nepali citizen.
  • Licensed guide: since 2023, and restated in February 2025, foreign trekkers in Langtang National Park are required to hire a licensed guide. Checks in Helambu are inconsistent, but hire one anyway. A local guide gets the cave caretakers and keys, books lodges, knows the base-camp road and is a second adult who knows the terrain. Your brother's Nepali is a big help, but he doesn't count as the guide.
  • Ask the agency to process permits and TIMS together. Many now want proof of travel insurance first.

Money & SIM

Dollars, rupees and a signal in the hills

Converter

Nepal Rastra Bank's buying rate was NPR 154.48 per dollar on 12 October 2026. Money changers and banks quote close to it. Edit the rate when you land.

How to handle US dollars

  • Bring crisp, recent, unmarked bills. Banks and changers reject torn or marked notes. Bring exact cash for visas, plus some $50s and $100s, which get the best rates.
  • Change money at banks or licensed money changers (Thamel and Boudha have many). Keep the receipts so you can change leftovers back. Exporting rupees is prohibited.
  • ATMs work, with limits. Expect NPR 20,000–35,000 per withdrawal and a local fee of NPR 500–850 each time, on top of your bank's fees. Withdraw the maximum each time. In April 2026, Nabil and NMB worked with foreign cards; Siddhartha and NIC Asia did not.
  • Decline "convert to dollars" when an ATM or card terminal offers it. That's dynamic currency conversion, and it adds a markup.
  • Card fees: hotels and some shops add about 3% for cards. Use cash for small purchases.
  • Helambu runs on cash. Don't count on an ATM past Melamchi Bazaar. Carry enough rupees for lodges, meals, guide and jeep, plus spare.
  • Tell your bank you're traveling before you go.

SIM cards: get Nepal Telecom for Helambu

Tarkeghyang (2023)NTC 4G good · Ncell none Melamchi Ghyang (2023)NTC 4G good · Ncell 1 bar Ama Yangri above base campLittle or no signal
  • Both carriers, Nepal Telecom (NTC) and Ncell, have counters in the airport arrivals hall. Bring your original passport; a passport photo and copy can speed things up.
  • For this trip, buy NTC. It's the network that works in Helambu.
  • Ncell sells tourist SIMs and eSIMs with unlimited data. Recent listings show TouristPro packs at NPR 595, 995 and 1,995; the 1,995 Voyager pack runs 28 days. It works well in the city but not in Tarkeghyang.
  • Your phone must be unlocked. If it takes an eSIM, you can keep your US line active for bank codes.
  • Download offline maps of the region (Organic Maps or Maps.me) before leaving Kathmandu.

Altitude

Altitude sickness and your daughter

Short answer on medication

This plan is "low risk," so preventive medication isn't usually indicated. Still, see her doctor before the trip.

Medical guidelines classify the risk of acute mountain sickness mainly by where you sleep. People with no history of altitude illness who sleep at 2,800 m or lower fall in the low-risk group. Your highest night is 2,600 m, and Ama Yangri is a day trip down to that same bed. For low-risk travelers, the CDC says preventive acetazolamide (Diamox) is not indicated. It suggests carrying a pain reliever for headaches and having acetazolamide on hand to treat early symptoms if needed.

What to do: book a travel-clinic or pediatrician visit 4–6 weeks out. Ask whether to carry a treatment prescription for her and for you. Her dose depends on her weight, so let the doctor set it, and mention any sulfa allergy. Ask about motion-sickness medicine for the jeep in the same visit.

What to know

  • The CDC says children are as susceptible as adults. Fitness doesn't protect anyone.
  • Symptoms usually start 2–12 hours after arriving higher, often during or after the first night. Day 5 evening and Day 6 morning are the watch window.
  • Altitude illness feels like a hangover: headache plus poor appetite, nausea, fatigue, dizziness or bad sleep.
  • Kids may show it as crankiness, not eating, pale skin or wanting to be carried.
  • If you or she has had altitude sickness before, tell the doctor. A past episode moves the same itinerary up a risk category.

Rules that keep it safe

  • With symptoms, go no higher. Rest at the same altitude until they're fully gone.
  • If symptoms get worse, go down. From Tarkeghyang the fastest way down is the jeep road toward Melamchi Bazaar, another reason to keep the jeep with you.
  • Don't use pain relievers to keep climbing through a headache.
  • Keep her drinking. Dehydration mimics altitude sickness.
  • The Day 6 rest day before the summit is built in for this reason. Keep it.

Go down now and get help

Confusion, stumbling or trouble walking a straight line, unusual drowsiness, breathlessness while resting, or a wet cough. These point to the rare serious forms (HACE, HAPE). Descend right away, with the jeep if possible, and call for help.

Traveling with a 7-year-old

What to plan around

Shots and a clinic visit

  • CDC recommends hepatitis A and typhoid for most travelers to Nepal; the risk of typhoid here is among the highest in the world. Make sure her routine shots, including MMR, are current.
  • Ask about rabies pre-exposure shots. Rabies is common in dogs in Nepal and post-exposure care is mainly in big towns.

Dogs and monkeys

  • Teach her one rule: don't touch any animal, however friendly. Helambu villages have dogs.
  • Any bite, scratch or lick on broken skin: wash with soap and running water for 15 minutes and get to a clinic the same day.

Food and water

  • Only bottled or treated water, including for brushing teeth. Bring a filter or purifier for the hills.
  • Hot, freshly cooked food is safest. Dal bhat and momos are easy wins.
  • Hand sanitizer before every meal.

The jeep ride

  • Five to six hours of winding dirt road each way. Seat her in the middle or front with a view of the road.
  • Light snacks, regular stops and a bag within reach.

Pace on the trail

  • Plan on her taking 1.5 times the adult times quoted here, plus snack stops.
  • Let her lead, give her a job (map reader, chorten counter) and set turnaround times before you start.
  • Pass mani walls and chortens on the left, walking clockwise. She'll pick it up fast.

Kathmandu streets

  • Many streets have no sidewalks and plenty of motorbikes. Hold hands in traffic.
  • Air can be dusty and smoggy, especially in winter. Pack kid-size masks.
  • Put a card in her pocket with your and your brother's Nepali numbers and the hotel name.

Temples and monasteries

  • Shoes off, shoulders and knees covered, walk clockwise.
  • Don't touch offerings or point feet at altars. Ask before photographing monks or people at prayer.
  • Teach her Namaste (hello) and Dhanyabad (thank you).

When to go

  • Best: October–November (clear skies) and March–April (rhododendrons in bloom).
  • December–February is clear but cold, and Ama Yangri can have snow.
  • June–September is monsoon: landslides and clouds. Avoid it.
  • Big festivals (Dashain, Tihar) fill jeeps and close offices, so check dates.
